The diagnosis
AI adoption is siloed and stagnant

Every company we talk to has the same story. They rolled out Copilot or ChatGPT for their developers. Maybe some non-tech people use it individually. Six months later, nothing has fundamentally changed. The AI hasn't compounded. The org hasn't transformed. This is the problem we exist to solve.

What's happening today

The Fragmented Adoption Reality

  • AI tools are a "tech team thing" — no one else feels responsible
  • Individual developers find prompting tricks that stay personal, never shared
  • Teams plateau after initial adoption — same results month after month
  • CEO talks about "AI strategy" but engineering has no connection to it
  • Business units don't know how to identify their AI opportunities
  • No measurement framework — "are we getting value?" is unanswerable
  • Each person re-learns what their colleague learned last week
  • New models, tools, and applications launch weekly — the adoption gap widens faster than teams can close it
What it should look like

Structured Organizational AI Transformation

  • A framework that covers every layer: CEO → BU → Tech → Individual
  • Individual learning flows into team knowledge automatically
  • Teams compound — each sprint better than the last
  • Executive vision connects directly to engineering execution
  • Every BU has an AI opportunity map tied to real processes
  • Clear metrics: maturity level, DORA, first-pass acceptance, ROI
  • A platform that makes the transformation self-sustaining
The root cause: The industry sold AI tools. Nobody sold AI transformation. Tools are necessary but not sufficient. What's missing is the methodology, the rituals, the measurement, the platform, and the human accompaniment from the C-suite to the code.

How we build
Design Principles

These principles govern both product and program decisions. They are the "why" behind every architectural and go-to-market choice.

01

Stage 2 visible from Day 1

The expanded organizational vision is always present in every conversation — with clients, candidates, and partners. We are not pivoting to Stage 2; we are executing Stage 1 toward Stage 2. Clients who buy Stage 1 should feel they are buying into a larger journey.

02

Architecture for the org, not the team

The Context Engine, agent design, and data model must support organization-level intelligence from the start. Designing only for team-level use today means an expensive refactor when Stage 2 arrives at the worst possible moment.

03

Compounding over coverage

We do not try to cover every phase superficially. We cover each phase deeply enough that the flywheel activates — so each engagement compounds on the previous one. A team that gets better every sprint is more valuable than a team that covered 8 phases and plateaued.

04

Nébula stays inside the client

The platform deploys within the client's own infrastructure. This is a feature, not a limitation. It builds trust with security-conscious enterprise buyers, eliminates data privacy objections, and makes the client's investment tangible and permanent.

05

Measurement is non-negotiable

Every engagement starts with a FINOS baseline assessment. Every engagement ends with measurable DORA movement. "We feel better about AI" is not an outcome. Specific numbers are. This protects Andino Labs' credibility and makes renewal conversations easy.

06

Program over product

Nébula alone does not transform an organization. The platform is inseparable from the 3-track program, the methodology, the rituals, and the human accompaniment. Selling the platform without the program is selling a tool — and we are not in the tool business.

The bet we are making: The industry will figure out the tooling layer (it already has). The lasting value is in the methodology, the flywheel, and the human transformation layer that tools can't replace. We are building the practice layer of AI transformation in LATAM — and eventually in the US.
